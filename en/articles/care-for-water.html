Water as a Common Good — Why We Need to Better Understand and Protect Water Resources

Water is fundamental to life

Water is one of the most important natural resources on Earth. It is essential for ecosystems, agriculture, economies and human societies. At the same time, its availability and quality are increasingly affected by climate change, human pressure and the way we manage the environment.

Pope Leo XIV's September prayer intention draws attention to the care of water and the need for just and sustainable management of this vital resource. It is an important reminder that protecting water is not only a technical challenge, but also a matter of responsibility for people and the environment.

Why does this matter for forests?

Forests are a key component of the hydrological system. They influence infiltration, water retention, evapotranspiration, soil moisture, surface and groundwater runoff, and water quality. Caring for water therefore cannot be limited to rivers, lakes or reservoirs — we also need to understand entire catchments and the role of forests in the water cycle.

Science as a tool for protecting water resources

Satellite data, GIS, remote sensing, field measurements and hydrological models allow us to analyse soil moisture, vegetation condition, water retention and water movement across space and time.

ForestHydrology connects forest hydrology with modern spatial data analysis. We want to better understand where water is located, how it moves through landscapes, how forests influence the water cycle, and how climate change and human activities alter these processes.

Effective protection of water resources requires not only awareness and responsibility, but also knowledge. We need to be able to measure, monitor and model water resources.
