Forest Hydrology is a set of research notes on how forested catchments intercept, store and release water — and how spatial data, remote sensing and modelling let us study that at scale.

The site collects short articles organised around five practical themes: **GIS** for spatial analysis of terrain and catchments, **Remote Sensing** for satellite and airborne observation, **Modelling** for hydrological and ecohydrological simulation, **Data** for open datasets and field measurements, and **WebGIS** for interactive, browser-based mapping tools.

## Why this site exists

Most resources on forest hydrology are either locked in journal paywalls or scattered across tool-specific documentation. This is an attempt to write the connective tissue: short, practical notes that link a method to the data it needs and the code that runs it.

## Languages

Content is written primarily in Polish, English and Spanish, with a Japanese section currently in testing. Not every article is available in every language yet — articles are translated as time allows, and each page links to the languages it's currently available in.
