# Forest hydrology — an introduction to water in forests

Forest hydrology examines how water is stored and transported through forest ecosystems and how forests influence hydrological processes. It includes precipitation, canopy interception, infiltration, soil water storage, surface and subsurface runoff, evapotranspiration, and groundwater dynamics.

## Why is forest hydrology important?

A forest is not simply a water consumer. Stand structure, litter, root systems, and soil properties influence how precipitation reaches the ground, infiltrates into the soil, and leaves a watershed.

In practice, understanding forest water dynamics requires combining field observations with meteorological, hydrological, and spatial data.

## What should we monitor?

Depending on the research question, useful variables include:

- soil moisture,
- groundwater levels,
- watershed runoff,
- evaporation and evapotranspiration,
- spatial and temporal precipitation variability,
- wetland and water-body extent,
- vegetation condition and water content.

Modern forest hydrology increasingly combines field observations with GIS, satellite remote sensing, UAV data, and spatial modelling.
