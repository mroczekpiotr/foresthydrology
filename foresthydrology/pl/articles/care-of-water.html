Water is often described as a resource, a model parameter or a variable on a map. Before it becomes a number in a water balance, however, it is a condition of life. The September 2026 “Pray with the Pope” intention on caring for water reminds us that water management is simultaneously an environmental, social and ethical issue.

From a global message to a catchment

GIS reveals catchment structure and drainage networks. Remote sensing helps observe surface water, moisture and vegetation change. SWAT+, HEC-HMS and HEC-RAS can be used to test scenarios. Field measurements tell us what is actually happening in the landscape.

Forests and water

Forests interact with precipitation interception, evapotranspiration, infiltration, runoff, groundwater recharge and storage. Caring for water in forests therefore requires looking at the whole system.

Good water management begins with good knowledge — and good knowledge begins with observation, measurement and the right questions.

The video is a starting point for a wider discussion about how science, forestry and environmental engineering can work together to protect water resources.
