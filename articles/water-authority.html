Why hydrology needs real authorities

In memory of Prof. Edward Pierzgalski · 4 September 2026
#EdwardPierzgalski#ForestHydrology#Hydrology#Melioration#Science

Today I attended the funeral of Prof. dr hab. inż. Edward Pierzgalski. I would like the first memorial post on Forest Hydrology to be more than a biographical note. His death is a moment to reflect on something that is increasingly important in science: the need for genuine authorities.

Authority is not the same as visibility

Scientific authority cannot be built simply by being visible, publishing frequently or speaking confidently. It is built over decades: through knowledge, research, teaching, difficult questions, responsibility for decisions and the ability to pass knowledge to younger generations.

This distinction matters especially in hydrology, forest hydrology, drainage and environmental engineering. These disciplines connect scientific theory with interventions in real landscapes. A wrong assumption can affect water resources, forests, agriculture, infrastructure and entire catchments.

A life connected with water and engineering

According to the official SGGW memorial notices, Professor Pierzgalski was born on 15 March 1944 in Gdynia. He graduated from SGGW in 1967 and began working at the university in 1968 in the Department of Agricultural and Forest Drainage, remaining at SGGW until his retirement in 2014. His professional experience included work related to water management and soil-erosion protection in Libya and Iraq, as well as international academic and research experience.

SGGW also records his service as Dean of the Faculty of Land Reclamation and Environmental Engineering in 1990–1993 and Vice-Rector for Teaching in 1993–1996. He was a specialist in hydrology, water management and environmental engineering, chaired the Polish Academy of Sciences Committee for Agricultural Environmental Engineering and Land Reclamation in 2002–2008, and held roles in ICID and the FAO European Forestry Commission working group on mountain watershed management.

Why this matters for forest hydrology

Forest hydrology sits at the intersection of disciplines that are sometimes separated institutionally: forestry, hydrology, soil science, drainage engineering, ecology, meteorology and spatial analysis. The strongest researchers are able to see the system as a whole.

That is why genuine mentors matter. They teach not only methods but also scientific judgement: when a model is appropriate, when a dataset is insufficient, when an elegant result is actually implausible, and when an engineering intervention solves one problem while creating another.

We need people whose authority comes from what they know, what they have done, what they have taught and what they have left behind in other people.

A challenge for the next generation

Forest Hydrology is being created at a time when remote sensing, GIS, machine learning and hydrological models make it possible to analyse landscapes at an extraordinary scale. But better tools do not automatically produce better science. They increase the responsibility of the scientist.

Perhaps the best way to remember Professor Pierzgalski is therefore not only to repeat his titles and achievements, but to continue the kind of serious, interdisciplinary thinking that those achievements represent.

May there be more researchers who become authorities because they have earned the trust of their students, colleagues and society — not because an algorithm made them visible.

In memoriam

Prof. dr hab. inż. Edward Pierzgalski
15 March 1944 – 26 August 2026

With gratitude for a generation of Polish hydrologists, engineers and scientists who built the foundations on which the next generation can work.
