The care of water begins with understanding its value

Piotr Mroczek · 4 September 2026
#Water#ForestHydrology#WaterManagement

Water is often discussed as a resource, a parameter in a model, or a variable on a map. Yet before it becomes a number in a hydrological balance, water is a condition of life. The September 2026 “Pray with the Pope” message places the care of water in this wider perspective and calls for just and sustainable management of a resource on which every community depends.

For those working with forests, catchments and environmental engineering, this message has a particularly practical dimension. Forest hydrology is ultimately about understanding where water comes from, where it goes, how long it remains in a system and how land management changes these pathways.

From a global message to a local catchment

Remote sensing can show changes in surface water. GIS can reveal drainage networks and catchment structure. Models such as SWAT+, HEC-HMS and HEC-RAS can help us test hydrological scenarios. Measurements of groundwater, soil moisture and streamflow can tell us what is happening on the ground.

None of these tools replaces responsibility. They make responsible decisions possible.

Forests and water

Forest ecosystems interact with precipitation, interception, evapotranspiration, infiltration, groundwater recharge, runoff and storage. The challenge is not to reduce these relationships to a single slogan, but to understand them well enough to make decisions appropriate to a particular site, catchment and climate.

Good water management begins with good knowledge — and good knowledge begins with observing, measuring and asking the right questions.
