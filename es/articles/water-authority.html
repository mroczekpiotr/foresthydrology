Por qué la hidrología necesita verdaderas autoridades

Piotr Mroczek · 4 September 2026
#ForestHydrology#Hydrology

Today I attended the funeral of Prof. Edward Pierzgalski. I want this memorial post to be more than a biography. It is a reflection on why forest hydrology, drainage engineering and water management need genuine authorities.

Authority is not visibility

Scientific authority is built over decades through knowledge, research, teaching, responsibility, difficult questions and the development of younger generations. It is not simply publication counts or public visibility.

A life connected with water and engineering

According to SGGW memorial materials, Prof. Pierzgalski was born on 15 March 1944 in Gdynia. He graduated from SGGW in 1967 and worked there from 1968, including in agricultural and forest drainage. He served as Dean of the Faculty of Land Reclamation and Environmental Engineering from 1990–1993 and Vice-Rector for Teaching from 1993–1996. He was a specialist in hydrology, water management and environmental engineering and was active in PAN, ICID and FAO.

Why this matters for forest hydrology

Forest hydrology sits at the intersection of forestry, hydrology, soil science, drainage engineering, ecology, meteorology and spatial analysis. We need people able to see the whole system.

We need people whose authority comes from what they know, what they have achieved, what they have taught and what they leave behind.

A challenge for the next generation

GIS, remote sensing, machine learning and hydrological models give us extraordinary analytical power. Better tools do not automatically produce better science. Scientific judgement, experience and responsibility remain essential.

The best way to remember Professor Pierzgalski is not only to repeat his achievements, but to continue serious interdisciplinary thinking about water.

Prof. Edward Pierzgalski
15 March 1944 – 26 August 2026
